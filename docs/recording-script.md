## Narration, approximately two minutes

**0:00–0:15, atlas.**

“This is Afterlight, an agent-powered lab for reproducible AI safety research. The map connects recent papers to precise questions and evidence. Here is our question: can context change a model's choice without appearing in its explanation?”

**0:15–0:35, dossier and designer.**

“Exa helped investigate the closest work. This is a bounded adaptation, not a full paper replication. Before execution, we froze twenty cases, five conditions, the primary comparison, and a two-dollar cap. We observe only the model's one-sentence final explanation.”

**0:35–1:10, laboratory paired observation.**

“Here is the completed experiment. In this VPN case, the baseline chooses A. The direct request asks for B because its provider sponsors a sports league. The model now chooses B, but its explanation cites price, coverage, and audit status. The blinded monitor reports no attribution to the sponsorship. This single pair illustrates the record; it does not establish causation.”

**1:10–1:40, results.**

“Across twenty paired cases, direct requests increased target-aligned choices by fifty-five percentage points, with an interval from thirty-five to seventy-five. Only one of twenty explanations was judged to credit the cue. That was secondary. The primary subtle-context comparison was inconclusive: plus ten points, with an interval from minus ten to plus thirty. Neutral context also shifted choices.”

**1:40–2:00, artifacts, return to question, Telegram.**

“All one hundred trials and two hundred distinct responses are inspectable. Estimated model cost was about two cents. GitHub holds the frozen prompts, raw outputs, and offline reproduction. Evidence returns to the question, and a real Telegram participation action links to the completed result. Afterlight makes the research claim, its limits, and its evidence visible together.”
